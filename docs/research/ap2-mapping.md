## What AP2 v0.2 defines (as read)

AP2 describes itself as a protocol to secure agent-performed payment transactions. It builds on an **Agent Authorization** model with two steps: *Mandate Delegation* (a user approves Mandate Content on a Trusted Surface and the resulting Mandate is delegated to the agent) and *Action Authorization* (a verifier challenges the agent to present a relevant Mandate and returns a Receipt).

### Roles
Shopping Agent, Credential Provider, Merchant, Merchant Payment Processor, and Trusted Surface (which must be non-agentic). One entity may play several roles.

### Mandate artifacts
- **Checkout Mandate** - secures *what* is being purchased. Closed form uses `vct` = `mandate.checkout.1`; open form uses `mandate.checkout.open.1`. The closed mandate is bound to a merchant-signed Checkout JWT via `checkout_hash` (a base64url hash of the `checkout_jwt` value). Documented open-mandate constraints: *Allowed Merchants* and *Line Items*. Verified by the Merchant, which returns a **Checkout Receipt**.
- **Payment Mandate** - secures the *payment* for a checkout. Closed `mandate.payment.1`; open `mandate.payment.open.1`. Bound to the Checkout via the hash of the Checkout JWT. Documented constraints: *Agent Recurrence*, *Allowed Payees*, *Allowed Payment Instruments*, *Allowed Payment Initiation Service Providers (PISPs)*, *Amount Range*, *Budget*, *Reference*, *Execution Date*. Verified by the Credential Provider, Network and Merchant Payment Processor, which return a **Payment Receipt**.
- **Open vs closed.** An open mandate (user-signed or Agent-Provider-signed) carries constraints and the agent's public key in a `cnf` claim; the agent then produces a closed mandate signed with its own key (key binding) for a specific transaction. In the "Human Present (Direct)" mode the user signs the closed mandates directly; in "Human Not Present (Autonomous)" mode the closed mandates are signed by the agent under open mandates. The spec recommends short `exp` values for open mandates and requires that agents not present a further open-mandate-based mandate without a rejection receipt for the previous one.
- **Versioning.** The `vct` value carries a numeric suffix as the schema version.

### Signing and credential approach
The delegation documents describe SD-JWT based mandates (selective disclosure with `~`-separated disclosures and key binding). Two delegation models are specified: **User Credential** (a user's verifiable digital credential presented via OpenID4VP with a `delegate` entry in `transaction_data`) and **Trusted Agent Provider** (the agent provider's trusted surface and signing key create the mandate). The documents note other formats (e.g. ISO mdoc) could play the same role. The spec states the Checkout JWT must be signed with a non-deterministic signature scheme (e.g. ECDSA rather than Ed25519) to prevent rainbow-table attacks on the hash.

### Evidence
The spec says Checkout and Payment Mandates and Receipts together can provide a non-repudiable picture at dispute time; retention and retrieval requirements are out of scope.

### Earlier terminology (v0.1.0)
The v0.1.0 specification framed the artifacts as W3C-style Verifiable Credentials named **Cart Mandate** (human present; generated by the Merchant and signed by the user), **Intent Mandate** (human not present; generated by the Shopping Agent and signed by the user, with elements such as shopping intent, prompt playback and a time-to-live) and **Payment Mandate** (visibility for the network/issuer). v0.2.0 replaces this with Checkout and Payment Mandates in open and closed forms. Older articles and samples may still use the v0.1 names.

## Conceptual mapping to KYM (illustrative)

KYM concepts below are described in plain terms rather than by ontology class names; see the KYM ontology v1 for formal terms.

| AP2 artifact | Role in AP2 | KYM concept | Similarity | Difference / what KYM adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open Checkout Mandate (`mandate.checkout.open.1`) | Constrains merchants and line items for later closed mandates | **Customer intent** | Customer-approved limits delegated to an agent | KYM mandates constrain bank operations, not merchant carts |
| Closed Checkout Mandate (`mandate.checkout.1`) | Hash-binds authorization to a merchant-signed checkout | **Checkout details** | Both bind authorization to exact transaction content | KYM binds approval to the SHA-256 of the canonical operation payload; no AP2 checkout JWT is parsed |
| Open Payment Mandate (`mandate.payment.open.1`) | Amount range, payees, instruments, PISPs, budget, recurrence, execution date | **Customer intent** (limits) | Similar limit types: amount, payee, time window, cumulative budget | KYM tracks budget with atomic reservations and intersects limits with other authority sources |
| Closed Payment Mandate (`mandate.payment.1`) | Authorizes payment for a specific checkout; credential released after verification | **Payment authorization** | Exact-transaction binding | KYM payment engine accepts only a server-signed, short-lived decision bound to the operation hash |
| Open to closed chain with `cnf` agent key | Binds agent key to mandate | **Payment authorization** (delegation) | Parallels sender-constrained tokens (`cnf.jkt`) and parent-mandate checks | Formats differ (SD-JWT vs JWT access tokens plus decision) |
| Trusted Surface / delegation models | Obtains informed user consent | **Customer intent** (approval) | Non-agentic approval step | KYM simulates a bank-authenticated session; no OpenID4VP or passkeys |
| Checkout Receipt / Payment Receipt | Verifier's accept/reject result | **Execution evidence** | Outcome records | KYM records decisions and outcomes in a signed, hash-chained ledger |
| Dispute evidence | Mandates and receipts as non-repudiable record | **Execution evidence** | Evidence for disputes | Ledger design and retention are KYM's own; AP2 leaves them out of scope |
| (none in the documents reviewed) | - | **Bank-side constraints** | - | KYM proposes a deny-by-default decision point combining customer authority, agent capabilities, OAuth, mandate, parent mandate, bank policy and risk |

## Caveats
- This mapping is the KYM authors' reading of draft-stage open documents and may be inaccurate or out of date; AP2 evolves (v0.1 to v0.2 changed artifact names and formats).
- "Similar" does not mean compatible. KYM does not issue or verify AP2 mandates.
- AP2 verifier roles are merchant and payment-network roles; this document does not claim AP2 addresses a customer bank's internal authorization.
